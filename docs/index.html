Sources, scope & the reach calculation +

Reviewed October 25, 2026. We count organizations with published privacy or sensitive-data restrictions on AI use, including conditional approvals. These are five documented examples, not a census of NC organizations or five blanket AI bans. Private companies, nonprofits, healthcare providers and public bodies qualify under the same definition when a source establishes their restriction; unverified organizations are not counted.

  • North Carolina Department of Information Technology ↗State guidance prohibits entering personal or confidential information into public generative AI. Counted once, without multiplying by covered agencies.
  • NC State University ↗Sensitive-data use depends on approved tools and, for other Red or Purple data uses, an IT compliance review.
  • North Carolina A&T State University ↗Confidential data is prohibited in generative AI; sensitive data requires approved tools and authorization.
  • Duke University ↗Only data classified as public may be submitted to publicly available AI tools. This university source does not cover Duke University Health System.
  • Central Piedmont Community College ↗Restricted or sensitive information cannot go into public or unapproved AI tools; approved uses still require appropriate review.

Community size: 39,259 NC State + 15,275 N.C. A&T + 17,325 Duke = 71,859 enrollments. Sources: UNC System fall 2025 preliminary report and Duke fall 2025 facts. Using the same enrollment year makes the figures comparable. This is a partial population proxy: it excludes employees and the other two organizations, may include cross-enrolled students, and does not measure actual restrictions experienced.

Illustrative reach: round(71,859 × 10%) = 7,186. Ten percent is an explicit planning assumption, not a surveyed adoption rate or forecast. The scenario assumes users have a suitable use case and permission to use Cloak with ChatGPT. It does not estimate people who can bypass a ban or achieve legal compliance; neither outcome has been established. None of these organizations is represented as a Cloak customer, partner or endorser.

    DESIGNED AROUND YOUR CONTROL
    Privacy, by design.
    How does the hospital example relate to HIPAA?+
    It demonstrates masking four identifier types in a fictional medical-history prompt. The medical context remains in the outgoing request. Cloak does not establish HIPAA compliance or guarantee removal of all protected health information. Healthcare deployment requires an organization’s privacy and security review. Read HHS de-identification guidance ↗
    What stays on my device?+
    Detection and token mappings stay local. The extension’s searchable vault supports individual reveal, export, and clear. Cloak has no server or telemetry; your AI service receives the masked request.
    What does Cloak cover?+
    ChatGPT text chat requests on Chrome 111+. Gemini and Claude are experimental and are not supported in this release. Detected image/video generation requests bypass masking and trigger a notice. Pattern detection can miss details; review sensitive prompts. Tokens are placeholders, not encryption.
    What am I testing on this page?+
    The expandable demos use the actual masking, restoration, stream-handling, and request-classification functions in a separate preview build. They process samples locally without starting an AI conversation or accessing extension data. The screenshot gallery documents a separate real ChatGPT conversation.
    What’s next for Cloak?+
    The roadmap includes a permission system where AI can request a specific detail only when needed—for example, to schedule a meeting—and the user decides whether to share it. This is planned functionality.
